Sample deliverable

Sample Analytics Clarity Audit

A fictional DTC storefront: measurement gaps and a prioritized action plan.

Illustrative sample. Fictional scenario and synthetic data, created to demonstrate deliverable structure. This is not a paid client engagement, a real audit, or evidence of business results.

Scenario: A fictional DTC storefront has conflicting purchase counts between its store backend and analytics tool. The owner wants to understand which metrics are safe to use before investing in a new dashboard.

Example scope: One storefront, one purchase journey, existing exports, and a review of KPI definitions. The output is a diagnostic report, a measurement map, and a prioritized improvement roadmap.

Executive summary

In this hypothetical scenario, revenue definitions and event coverage need reconciliation before the team uses the funnel for planning. The sample recommendations below describe checks to perform, not verified findings about any business.

Example measurement review
AreaHypothetical observationEvidence to requestRecommended check
Purchase countStore orders and analytics purchases differ.Transaction IDs, order statuses, and purchase event exports.Compare the same date range and time zone; check missing and duplicate transaction IDs.
Revenue definitionTwo reports may treat refunds and shipping differently.Metric glossary and refund records.Document gross vs. net revenue and exclusions before comparing reports.
Funnel stepsCheckout events may not be consistent across devices.Event specification and device-level event counts.Check event coverage with the implementation owner before interpreting drop-off.

Prioritized roadmap

  1. First: agree KPI definitions. Expected impact: clearer comparisons. Effort: low. Owner: business decision maker.
  2. Next: reconcile transaction IDs. Expected impact: more credible purchase reporting. Effort: medium. Owner: analytics or implementation team.
  3. Then: review funnel coverage. Expected impact: more interpretable journey analysis. Effort: medium. Owner: website implementation team.

Handoff and limitations

The final diagnostic would include the reviewed sources, definitions, assumptions, open questions, evidence limitations, and recommended next steps. Production fixes, tag deployment, live experiment operations, and continuous monitoring are outside this example scope. Priorities would change if actual evidence contradicted these assumptions.
